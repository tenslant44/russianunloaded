RUSSIAN ROYALE
GAME DOCUMENTATION

This guide describes the game as it is currently implemented in the project.


1. GAME OVERVIEW

Russian Royale is a single-player, third-person battle royale played against
AI-controlled characters. The player drops from a flying battle bus, searches
the map for weapons and supplies, fights other teams, and tries to be part of
the last team left alive.

The game has:
- Solo, Duos, and Squads team sizes.
- Classic and Reload match rules.
- Four environments, with a random-map option.
- Procedurally placed terrain details, houses, and loot.
- Shooting, melee, destructible objects, and player-built cover.
- Health, shields, injuries, bleeding, healing, and a shrinking storm.
- Rideable polar bears on the Siberia map.
- Stylized, graphic gore, ragdoll bodies, sound effects, and radio chatter.

Duos and Squads are teams of bots around the player. The current project does
not connect to online players or provide multiplayer networking.


2. STARTING A MATCH

Before a match, choose:

TEAM
- Solo: 25 total combatants (the player and 24 bots).
- Duos: 26 total combatants, arranged in teams of two.
- Squads: 24 total combatants, arranged in teams of four.

MODE
- Classic: one life per combatant.
- Reload x3 Life: three lives per combatant. After a death, a living player
  with a life remaining returns after five seconds.

MAP
- Island
- Siberia
- Pripyat
- Baikonur Steppe
- Random: selects one of the four maps for the match.

The battle bus follows a randomly selected route across the map for about
30 seconds. Press Space to jump when ready. If the player has not jumped by
the end of the route, the game drops them automatically. Teammate bots follow
the player's drop; other bots choose their own drop times and landing areas.

At match start, the player has 100 health, no shield, 60 wood, and a pickaxe.
The other four inventory slots are empty. Find weapons and supplies on the
ground or inside houses. Each map gets 70 scattered ground-loot items, plus
loot placed inside its houses.


3. CONTROLS

KEYBOARD AND MOUSE
- W, A, S, D: move.
- Mouse: look around while pointer lock is active.
- Left mouse button: fire or use the selected item. Hold for automatic guns;
  single-fire weapons fire once per click.
- Right mouse button: aim with a gun. Aiming tightens weapon spread. Scoped
  weapons zoom in and use the scope view.
- Space: jump; jump from the battle bus while aboard.
- Left Shift: sprint while moving forward. It also releases crouch.
- C: toggle crouch. While in build mode, C selects a ramp instead.
- R: reload the selected gun.
- E: pick up nearby loot, mount a nearby polar bear, or dismount.
- 1 through 5: select the matching inventory slot.
- Mouse wheel: cycle through occupied inventory slots.
- V: toggle first-person view. Scoped aiming also enters first-person view.
- Q: toggle build mode.
- Z: enter build mode and select a wall.
- X: enter build mode and select a floor.
- C while building: select a ramp.

Click the game to capture the mouse if pointer lock is not active. The default
view is third person.

TOUCH CONTROLS
- Drag the left virtual stick to move.
- Drag on the game view to look around.
- FIRE: fire or use the selected item.
- AIM: aim with the selected gun.
- JUMP: jump or jump from the battle bus.
- USE: pick up loot, mount/dismount a bear, or reload when no nearby use action
  is available.
- BUILD: toggle build mode.
- Tap a hotbar slot to select it. Tap a build-bar choice to select a structure.


4. MATCH FLOW AND WINNING

After landing, explore, gather loot, fight, and move toward the safe area.
Direct attacks do not hurt living members of the same team. Bots search for
loot, follow the player's team leader when appropriate, pursue opponents they
can see, and can build defensive pieces when attacked.

The HUD's ALIVE counter counts player and bot combatants that are alive or
still have lives remaining. ELIMS shows the player's eliminations. The
killfeed reports deaths. If the player is eliminated while a teammate is
still alive, the player can spectate that teammate. The last team remaining
wins.

In Reload mode, a respawn takes five seconds and starts the combatant in the
air with full health, no shield, a pickaxe, and a randomly selected basic gun
(AR, SMG, or shotgun). Inventory is reset on respawn. Wood and kill counts
are not reset by the respawn code.


5. HEALTH, SHIELDS, DAMAGE, AND INJURIES

Players have up to 100 health and 100 shield. The player starts with 100
health and 0 shield. For ordinary damage, shield is consumed before health.
Unabsorbed damage reaches health. Healing cannot raise either bar above 100.

Hit locations change incoming damage:
- Head: twice the normal damage.
- Torso: normal damage.
- Arms and legs: 85% of normal damage.

Shotgun and Obrez pellets also lose damage with distance. Higher weapon
rarity increases weapon damage. Moving, airborne firing, aiming, crouching,
weapon spread, and recoil affect accuracy.

Damage can create wounds and bleeding. Bleeding drains health over time and
can be fatal. Bandages and medkits stop bleeding when their use completes.
Repeated injuries can crack the skull, open the torso, break legs, or sever
limbs. Missing or broken legs reduce movement speed; missing arms make gun
spread worse. Hard falls can damage health, cause bleeding, and break legs.

Deaths leave bodies that can continue to be hit or damaged by explosions.
Explosions can hurt combatants, destroy structures and trees, and break up
corpses.

POLAR BEAR DAMAGE WHILE RIDING
- Incoming damage is applied to the ridden bear's health first.
- If an attack defeats the bear, only the remaining damage passes to the
  rider.
- The rider's shield absorbs that remaining damage before the rider's health.
- The bear has 260 health and throws off its rider when it dies.
- Explosions and storm damage count a mounted bear and rider as one damage
  event, so the same area hit is not applied twice.


6. THE STORM

The storm begins after the battle bus finishes its route. It periodically
shrinks toward a new safe circle. Combatants outside the current circle take
storm damage about once per second; shields absorb storm damage before health.
The minimap shows the current storm and the next circle.

Storm schedule:
Phase 1: wait 45 seconds, shrink for 35 seconds to radius 190; 1 damage/second.
Phase 2: wait 35 seconds, shrink for 30 seconds to radius 110; 2 damage/second.
Phase 3: wait 30 seconds, shrink for 25 seconds to radius 55; 5 damage/second.
Phase 4: wait 25 seconds, shrink for 20 seconds to radius 22; 8 damage/second.
Phase 5: wait 20 seconds, shrink for 20 seconds to radius 0; 10 damage/second.


7. WEAPONS AND INVENTORY

The inventory has five slots. Slot 1 starts with the pickaxe. When all slots
are full, picking up an item replaces the currently selected item and drops
the replaced item, unless the pickaxe slot is selected. Matching healing
items stack together.

Weapon stats below are base values before rarity and hit-location modifiers.
Shotgun and Obrez damage is listed per pellet. Fire interval is the time
between shots.

PICKAXE
- 22 damage; 0.42-second interval; melee.
- Can hit combatants, trees, and structures.
- A hit on a tree awards 10 wood. A hit on a wood structure awards 5 wood;
  a hit on another structure awards 8 wood.

KALASHNIKOV (AR)
- 30 damage; 0.14-second interval; 30-round magazine; 2.2-second reload.
- Range 300; automatic.

BIZON SMG
- 17 damage; 0.075-second interval; 30-round magazine; 2-second reload.
- Range 120; automatic.

PUMP SHOTGUN
- 11 damage per pellet; 10 pellets; 0.95-second interval.
- 5-round magazine; 4-second reload; range 45.

DRAGUNOV
- 105 damage; 1.3-second interval; 5-round magazine; 3-second reload.
- Range 600; scoped zoom.

MAKAROV
- 24 damage; 0.2-second interval; 8-round magazine; 1.4-second reload.
- Range 90.

PKM MACHINE GUN (LMG)
- 26 damage; 0.09-second interval; 100-round magazine; 5.5-second reload.
- Range 280; automatic.

OBREZ SAWED-OFF
- 13 damage per pellet; 14 pellets; 0.35-second interval.
- 2-round magazine; 2.4-second reload; range 28.

RPG-7
- 160 base damage; 1.2-second firing interval; one rocket before reloading.
- 3.4-second reload; listed range 400.
- Rockets travel forward and explode on impact. Explosions damage nearby
  combatants, structures, and trees.

Healing and shield items:

SHIELD VODKA
- Restores 50 shield over 2.2 seconds.
- Starts with two uses per pickup.

MEDKIT
- Restores up to 100 health over 4 seconds and stops bleeding.
- Starts with one use per pickup.

BANDAGE
- Restores 15 health over 1.6 seconds and stops bleeding.
- Starts with five uses per pickup.

BABUSHKA PELMENI
- Restores 30 health and 10 shield over 2.6 seconds.
- Starts with three uses per pickup.

Taking non-storm damage interrupts an item in use. Storm damage does not
interrupt the use timer. The item is consumed when its use timer completes.

RARITY
- Common: gray; base damage.
- Uncommon: green; +7% weapon damage.
- Rare: blue; +14% weapon damage.
- Epic: purple; +21% weapon damage.
- Legendary: orange; +28% weapon damage.

Rarity is rolled when loot is created. Approximate roll chances from the
current code are Common 38%, Uncommon 28%, Rare 20%, Epic 10%, Legendary 4%.


8. BUILDING AND DESTRUCTIBLE OBJECTS

The player begins with 60 wood. Enter build mode with Q, then choose a wall,
floor, or ramp. Each piece costs 10 wood. Pieces snap to a four-unit grid.
New wood pieces start with 40 health and build up to 150 health over time.
Structures can block movement and shots and can be destroyed by weapons or
explosions.

Trees and rocks block movement. Trees can be destroyed; the pickaxe is the
main way to gain wood from them. Houses are built from destructible floor,
brick-wall, roof, and (in some houses) ramp pieces. Houses are placed around
the map with a random door direction.


9. POLAR BEARS

Nine polar bears spawn on Siberia. The other map presets do not spawn bears.
Wild bears wander and may become hostile to nearby combatants. When a bear is
attacked, it can target the attacker. A bear attacks at close range with a
claw maul.

To ride one:
1. Approach an unoccupied living bear.
2. Press E or touch USE when the ride prompt appears.
3. Steer with movement controls. Hold Shift to make the bear sprint; Space
   makes it jump.
4. Press E or USE again to dismount.

While mounted, the player can still use weapons and items. The bear's health
acts as the first layer of protection from incoming damage, followed by the
rider's shield and health as described above.


10. MAPS

Terrain, lighting, sky, fog, vegetation, rocks, house positions, loot, and
some set pieces are generated or positioned for each match. All maps use
the same storm and core combat rules.

ISLAND
- Bright green island with a central mountain.
- 420 trees, 90 rocks, and 16 houses.
- No special landmark or polar bears.

SIBERIA
- Snowy tundra, pine trees, falling snow, and colder lighting.
- 520 trees, 110 rocks, and 14 houses.
- Contains a giant matryoshka landmark and nine rideable polar bears.

PRIPYAT
- Muted, overgrown environment with falling ash and an abandoned Ferris wheel.
- 300 trees, 70 rocks, and 26 houses. Many houses have two floors.
- Some trees appear dead. The ash is an environmental visual effect; storm
  rules still provide the map's outside-circle damage.

BAIKONUR STEPPE
- Warm-colored, dry terrain with drifting dust and a Soyuz-style rocket
  landmark.
- 110 trees, 150 rocks, and 18 houses.


11. BOTS AND TEAMS

Bots choose loot, pick weapons based on their target's distance, reload,
heal when safe, move toward the safe area, and engage opponents they can see.
They strafe and sometimes jump while fighting. Bots can build a wall and
sometimes a ramp after taking damage.

In Duos and Squads, teammates share a team number and do not directly damage
one another. Bots on the player's team can follow the player after the drop.
Enemies get floating name and health tags when close; teammates can be tagged
at greater distances when visible.


12. HUD AND FEEDBACK

The match HUD can show:
- Minimap, player direction, battle-bus route, current storm, and next storm.
- Number of remaining combatants, player eliminations, and killfeed.
- Health, shield, bleeding warning, and damage numbers.
- Selected inventory, ammunition, reload/use progress, and wood count.
- Team health, player lives in Reload mode, interaction prompts, and storm
  timer.
- Radio Moscow messages, kill streak callouts, damage flash, and blood on the
  screen.

The player's camera starts in third person. V switches between third and
first person. Scoped weapons enter the scope view while aiming.


13. CONTENT AND AUDIO

The game displays an 18+ warning. Its violence effects include visible
wounds, blood sprays and pools, broken bones, severed limbs, exposed organs,
head injuries, explosions, and ragdoll corpses.

Audio includes gunfire, rocket and explosion sounds, melee impacts, building,
flesh impacts, screams, crunches, and splats. Audio is initialized after the
player presses Play. The sound files are stored in the project's sfx folder.


14. PROJECT FILE GUIDE

The project is a static browser game with no build step. index.html loads
style.css and js/main.js. Three.js is imported from a CDN through the HTML
import map; interface fonts are loaded from Google Fonts.

MAIN GAME CODE
- js/main.js: renderer setup, menu selections, match setup, game loop, storm,
  respawns, teams, and match-end flow.
- js/player.js: player controls, movement, camera, inventory interactions,
  building, touch controls, and mounted movement.
- js/combat.js: hit detection, weapons firing, explosions, health, shields,
  deaths, corpses, and damage handling.
- js/bots.js: AI movement, looting, aiming, combat, healing, and bot building.
- js/bears.js: polar bear model, AI, maul attack, riding, and bear damage.
- js/weapons.js: weapon/item statistics, rarity, item models, and floor loot.
- js/maps.js: map presets and random map selection.
- js/world.js: procedural terrain, trees, rocks, landmarks, and ambient
  weather effects.
- js/structures.js: build pieces, house generation, collisions, and structure
  damage.
- js/hud.js: minimap, status bars, messages, tags, damage numbers, and
  end-of-match display.

CHARACTERS, EFFECTS, AND SUPPORT
- js/character.js: character models, animation, wounds, injuries, and outfit
  definitions.
- js/ragdoll.js: body physics after death.
- js/gore.js: blood, body parts, particles, and corpse effects.
- js/goretex.js: generated textures and materials for gore effects.
- js/fx.js: tracers, muzzle flashes, explosions, puffs, and shield sparks.
- js/audio.js: local sound loading and playback.
- js/state.js: shared match state.
- js/util.js: shared math, random, geometry, and material helpers.
- sfx/: WAV sound assets used by the audio module.
